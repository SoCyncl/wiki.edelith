:::hatnote
Surviving accounts of Woodsworn origin and history conflict significantly. This article presents each attributed account rather than reconciling them. For Damien Zerxes' study, see [[The Woodsworn]]. For Lucinda Glasc's memoir, see [[Among the Woodsworn]].
:::
The Woodsworn are a race of wooden automaton constructs given life by the Goddess [[Hiebrief]][^zerxes][^woodsworn]. Two principal firsthand accounts of their history survive: that of [[Lucinda Glasc]], the last Cremator, recorded in [[Among the Woodsworn]][^woodsworn]; and that of an anonymous individual referred to as "the Curator," recorded by the researcher Damien Zerxes in [[The Woodsworn]][^zerxes]. The two accounts differ substantially in detail and are not easily reconciled.

## Origins
According to Glasc, the Woodsworn were made in two distinct forms by Hiebrief's hand: the Cremators, of marble with porcelain masks, under [[Ossua]]; and the Rose Troupe, of wood, under [[Fatuo]] — together forming the faction [[Sub Rosa]][^woodsworn]. According to the Curator's account, by contrast, all Woodsworn were made of wood and served together as the Trickster's Troupe, a travelling circus that doubled as Hiebrief's assassins during the Great Schism[^zerxes]. Both accounts agree that Hiebrief eventually vanished without explanation, leaving the Woodsworn without purpose[^woodsworn][^zerxes].

## The Ring of Rotcio
The Curator's account describes [[Rotcio]] as a jester who betrayed the Woodsworn: while camping in the woods, Rotcio was approached by a Beast and struck a bargain granting him immortality as the Corrosion, jumping into the bark of trees, in exchange for stripping himself of physical form and abandoning the rest of the Woodsworn to scars and decaying wood[^zerxes]. Every Woodsworn is said to bear a resulting black ring in their bark, "the Ring of Rotcio," a source of recurring nightmares[^zerxes].

:::source[Miswak, The Saints of the Corrosion]
A puppet crafted from the Shrouded Goddess of Deceit, Hiebrief, the jester wished more from life than his mundane serfdom. So, within the darkness of night, the Grand Beast approached Rotcio. Rotcio's silver tongue worked as he condemned his entire race to be the first fauna vessels of Corrosion, servants to the Beast. In exchange, the clown received immortality as a black stain on all oak. Thus, the Woodsworn's fates were shackled by the Ring of Rotcio.
:::

This is corroborated in broad strokes by [[Miswak]]'s [[The Saints of the Corrosion]], which venerates Rotcio as the "Saint of Decay" among the [[Grand Beast]]'s followers[^saints]. Glasc's account of Rotcio's departure is comparatively gentle by contrast, describing it as "saddening" and recalling a day spent happily in his and [[Avao Woodsworn|Avao]]'s company, with no mention of betrayal or the Corrosion[^woodsworn].

## New Arvegum
Per the Curator, surviving Woodsworn eventually settled in New Arvegum, most serving as its guard: dressed in the robes of the hero Kojin, they slay demons outside the city walls but are never permitted within them, kept "out-of-mind" by a chest-mounted mechanism that feeds them fumes of Enfernabloom[^zerxes]. Zerxes attributes this arrangement to an encounter with an unnamed traveller in the service of the "Son of Kojin," who offered the Woodsworn a smoke that quieted their nightmares for a night; on reaching New Arvegum, they found the same substance and entered permanent service for an unlimited supply[^zerxes].

## Saoglas
A separate branch of Woodsworn, led by [[Fatuo]] following Hiebrief's disappearance, settled at [[Saoglas]], built around a tree grafted from the remains of Hiebrief's avatar[^woodsworn]. After Fatuo's departure into the [[Umbra]], [[Avao Woodsworn]] took up leadership of the settlement until its collapse[^woodsworn].

The settlement's fall is attributed by Glasc to an unnamed woman afflicted with corrosion who was taken in by the Woodsworn there[^woodsworn]. Her identity and motive are given directly in [[Letter to Avao]], a letter by [[Laura Passerine]] delivered to Avao by "the boy" at her request. In it, Laura confesses to killing Avao's people, states the corrosion will soon claim her in turn, and describes having shaped the boy in hopes he might carry "some fragment" of Avao — an echo of his temper and light — though she concludes "the boy is not you. He never was"[^letter-avao]. She also references a spirit called [[Holly]], whose bark and leaves form the boy's mask and coat, and who "remains still" despite the boy calling on her power[^letter-avao].

## Notable Woodsworn
- [[Ossua]] — Mask Maker and leader of the Cremators[^woodsworn].
- [[Fatuo]] — Mask Maker and leader of the Rose Troupe, later founder of [[Saoglas]][^woodsworn].
- [[Lucinda Glasc]] — the last surviving Cremator, author of [[Among the Woodsworn]][^woodsworn].
- [[Rotcio]] — jester whose bargain with the [[Grand Beast]] marked all Woodsworn with the Ring of Rotcio[^zerxes][^saints].
- [[Avao Woodsworn]] — leader of the now-defunct [[Saoglas]][^woodsworn].
- [[Herb Passerine]] — a Woodsworn of note among the present-day company; shares a surname with [[Laura Passerine]], author of [[Letter to Avao]], though no source confirms a relation.
- [[Miswak]] — a false angel formerly of the Rose Troupe, author of [[The Saints of the Corrosion]][^saints].
