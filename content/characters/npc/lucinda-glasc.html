Lucinda Glasc is the last surviving Cremator — one of two orders of constructed angels made by the goddess Hiebrief to serve the assassination faction Sub Rosa. Where the Rose Troupe dealt in swift and expendable death, the Cremators were reserved for the highest class of target: those whose end Hiebrief deemed worthy of ceremony.

She is, as far as any record shows, the only Woodsworn to have survived the fall of Saoglas intact. She left before the end. She has been writing since.

Identity & Nature

Lucinda is a constructed being — made, not born. Hiebrief crafted two distinct angel types as deliberate extremes: one for Sorrow, one for Joy, so that neither could achieve full self-awareness. An emotion so total it crowds out thought was the design philosophy.

editor's note

Lucinda's own account uses the phrase "built incapable of knowing yourself." Whether this design actually succeeded — whether she was constrained in ways she cannot perceive — remains one of the open questions in this record.

Cremators were tall, marble-bodied, female-presenting, and named with an -a suffix. Their porcelain masks were fragile and mouthless — unable to approximate human expression. Because the mask could carry nothing, the body carried everything: luminescence, stored information, identity. They were, as Lucinda describes it, statues.

The Rose Troupe were their mirror: short, wooden-masked, male-presenting, named with an -o suffix. Their masks could open and move. They were built to be repaired and treated as expendable. Lucinda calls this Hiebrief's little mirrors of each other — a symmetry she says she did not understand until much later.

Sub Rosa

Sub Rosa was the faction that housed all Woodsworn — both the Cremators under Ossua and the Rose Troupe under Fatuo — operating as Hiebrief's instrument for assassination and spectacle.

The Cremators

The Cremators did not do quick kills. Their work was ceremonial murder: the target bound, loved ones forced to witness, a proper funeral conducted before the end. The coffin was buried with the victim inside it — alive long enough to know they were mourned, to know who came. Lucinda considers this the kinder method.

The Rose Troupe

The Rose Troupe handled parties, entertainment, discreet performances — and assassination. Anyone Hiebrief did not deem worthy of the ceremony went to them. They were the majority of Sub Rosa's work.

Ossua's Rebellion

Ossua chose to rebel. She attempted to untangle herself from Hiebrief's control — to find something better for the women in her keeping. Hiebrief sent her own avatar to answer it.

The ceremony Ossua had performed on so many others was performed on her. Her porcelain mask displayed, mourned, and cremated. — Lucinda Glasc, Among the Woodsworn

After Ossua's death, the Rose Troupe was ordered to hunt the remaining Cremators. One by one, mask by mask, bodies destroyed past any repair. Lucinda watched every one of her sisters go. She was the sole exception.

It is theorized that Lucinda survived specifically because Hiebrief wanted a witness — someone to carry the record of what was done, and therefore what was owed. The mathematics of cruelty suggest preservation was not mercy.

Saoglas

After Hiebrief vanished, Fatuo gathered those who remained and led them beyond Edelith's walls to a place he named Saoglas, built around a tree grafted from the remains of Hiebrief's avatar. The avatar had continued commanding the Rose Troupe in her absence — they destroyed it together upon arrival.

Post-Wake
Saoglas founded

Fatuo leads surviving Woodsworn out of Edelith; builds settlement around Hiebrief's avatar-tree. Mycelium Network established to maintain contact between scattered factions.

Saoglas era
The Fracture

Arguments over purpose. Fatuo allows factions to split rather than force false peace. Ringo departs on good terms; Rotcio's departure mourned heavily by Lucinda, Avao, and Fatuo.

Saoglas era
Fatuo enters the Umbra

Takes Hiebrief's tree — the last source of wood for new masks — into the Umbra. Officially searching for the truth of the Wake of Steel and the lying mother. Lucinda believes he was searching for a way to bring Ossua back.

Post-Umbra
Miswak returns

What returned called itself Miswak. Sells the sacred wood to travelers for stories. Lucinda holds this as a profound betrayal. What Fatuo found in the Umbra, or what it cost him, is unknown.

End of Saoglas
Corrosion takes Saoglas

A woman with corrosion came to stay; the Woodsworn believed themselves immune. They were not. Saoglas falls. Lucinda leaves before the end. Avao does not survive.

On Miswak

The entity now calling itself Miswak is recognized by Lucinda as what returned from the Umbra in Fatuo's form. It sells the sacred wood — the only material from which new Woodsworn masks can be made — to travelers in exchange for stories. Lucinda considers this a desecration. The Woodsworn graft the masks of their fallen to themselves; to sell those faces as cheap decoration is an unforgivable corruption of that practice.

Theories
The Corrosion was deliberate

The woman who arrived at Saoglas and is blamed for the corrosion may have been sent, not wandered. The timing — arriving shortly after Miswak's return from the Umbra — suggests coordination. Whether Miswak engineered the destruction of Saoglas to remove rivals for the sacred wood, or whether the corrosion arrived by chance, remains unknown.

Lucinda's account is partial

Lucinda's account is written to remember Ossua. She states this plainly. That means the document carries a perspective that is protective of Ossua's memory and harsh toward those Lucinda holds responsible for the fall — Miswak and the woman with corrosion most prominently. Miswak's own letter, recovered separately, presents a materially different account of the same events and holds no guilt for any of it.
