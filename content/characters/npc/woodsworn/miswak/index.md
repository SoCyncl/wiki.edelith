Miswak is a false angel, formerly [[Fatuo]] of the [[Rose Troupe]] and [[Sub Rosa]], a woodsworn faction devoted to the Goddess [[Hiebrief]][^woodsworn]. Its current name dates from the entity's return from the [[Umbra]], which it entered searching for "the lying mother" following the collapse of [[Saoglas]][^woodsworn]. Miswak now travels the world selling fragments of the sacred wood — the last pure source from which new woodsworn masks could once be made — to travelers in exchange for stories[^woodsworn][^letter].

## Identity
It has never been confirmed that Miswak and [[Fatuo]] are the same individual. The only surviving account linking them is that of [[Lucinda Glasc]], the last Cremator, who records that Fatuo left [[Saoglas]] carrying [[Hiebrief]]'s tree and walked into the [[Umbra]] searching for "the lying mother" and for what became of the gods during the [[Wake of Steel]] — and that "what returned from the Umbra called itself Miswak"[^woodsworn]. Glasc speculates that Fatuo's underlying motive was to find a way to bring [[Ossua]] back. Miswak's own account, the [[Besotted Letter]], never confirms or denies this, referring only to "my creator, the lying mother — lady of wood"[^letter].

## The sacred wood
:::source[Lucinda Glasc, Among the Woodsworn]
What returned from the Umbra called itself Miswak. It sells the wood, the sacred wood, to travelers for stories and small entertainments. I do not know what it found in the dark. I do not know what it lost.
:::
To the surviving woodsworn, the sale of the wood is considered a profound betrayal. The wood is used to graft the masks of the fallen so that "their bark may continue to aid" the living[^woodsworn], and its sale is regarded as reducing the dead to "cheap decoration"[^woodsworn]. None among the remaining woodsworn have been able to stop it: by Glasc's account, Miswak possesses "absurd skill in combat"[^woodsworn].

## Avao and the boy
The [[Besotted Letter]] describes Miswak fashioning a mask — referred to only as "the boy" — from the essence of [[Avao Woodsworn|Avao]] after Avao's death. Miswak describes the boy as an incomplete vessel, lacking true "lumin-essence," and claims sole possession of his mind: "The boy's mind is mine, and mine alone — his thoughts and experiences, mine, mine alone"[^letter]. According to the letter, [[Rotcio]] attempted to repair the boy's "patchwerk mind," and the boy has since taken to consuming sullied bark in the belief it will restore him, an outcome Miswak dismisses as impossible[^letter]. Miswak refers to the boy as "my ledger keeper, my second eye. My fool," and states he now wanders the world as an informant[^letter].

## The woman at the pyre
The [[Besotted Letter]] opens with an account of an unnamed woman Miswak encountered "near a pyre of... my friends," whom it accuses of responsibility for their deaths: "It was her fault. She killed them"[^letter]. Miswak describes her as "too ridden with guilt to kill herself, too stricken with pride to admit it was her fault," and states she mourned "her twin flame"[^letter]. The letter does not name her, and no corroborating identification survives in [[Among the Woodsworn]].

## Personality and reliability
:::quote[Miswak, the Besotted Letter]
Through only the aggregation of stories is the truth revealed.
:::
Miswak's own testimony is erratic, self-contradictory, and openly gleeful about the suffering it describes, alternating between grief, mania, and laughter within single sentences. It professes to "collect stories" as a means of undoing "Hiebrief's lies," yet its account of its own history — including its relationship to [[Fatuo]], [[Avao Woodsworn|Avao]], and the woman it blames for the deaths at [[Saoglas]] — is fragmentary and difficult to verify[^letter]. For this reason its claims are treated here as apocryphal.
